Head-mounted wearables (HMWs) include True Wireless Stereo (TWS) earphones, VR/AR headsets, and smart glasses. These devices are equipped with microphones, IMUs, and other sensors that support applications such as voice interaction, motion recognition, and spatial audio. TWS earphones such as the Apple AirPods series are a major part of this market, with HMW shipments estimated at more than $273$ million units worldwide in 2023~\citep{wearable-stat}.
Many HMWs, especially earphones and headphones, support active noise cancellation (ANC) to improve listening. Voice-related applications based on HMW microphones, such as phone calls and voice assistants, are also widely used.

However, speech quality on HMWs remains limited for several reasons. First, most HMWs use omnidirectional microphones, which capture environmental noise from multiple directions.
Second, although many HMWs include multiple microphones for beamforming, the microphones are often too close to provide strong spatial resolution.
Third, speech is attenuated before it reaches the device because HMW microphones are usually far from the user's mouth.
Finally, masks and other face coverings can further reduce speech intensity at the microphone.

% Existing approach
Various approaches have been developed for speech enhancement. Signal-processing methods \citep{ephraim1995signal} suppress noise based on statistical models, but they are less effective in complex acoustic environments. Microphone beamforming \citep{yousefian2014hybrid,ji2017coherence} suppresses noise based on direction, but compact HMW microphone layouts limit spatial resolution. Deep neural networks (DNNs) have also been used to improve speech quality \citep{subakan2021attention, zhang2021microphone, chatterjee2022clearbuds}, although their performance can degrade under domain shift.
Beyond audio-only solutions, prior work has used contact sensors \citep{gupta2018precision}, vibration sensors \citep{maruri2018v}, cameras \citep{ gao2021visualvoice}, mmWave radar \citep{ozturk2021radiomic, li2020vocalprint, liu2021wavoice}, ultrasonic sensing \citep{sun2021ultrase, zhang2021sensing}, and LiDAR \citep{sami2020spying}. These approaches often require additional hardware or user effort beyond existing HMW capabilities.

% Technical Challenges
An inertial measurement unit (IMU) is a common sensor on HMWs and can capture speech-related vibration when mechanically coupled to the speaker's body \citep{ba2020learning, wang2021audio}. Because HMWs are attached to the user's head or ears, the IMU accelerometer can sense vibrations caused by the user's voice through skull bone conduction. This bone-conducted signal is user-specific and largely free of environmental noise and competing speakers. However, HMW accelerometers have limited sampling rates (e.g., $1.6~\text{kHz}$ in our prototype) due to size and energy constraints, which is insufficient to recover full-band speech (e.g., up to $3.4~\text{kHz}$ \citep{Voicefre75:online}).

This chapter presents \textit{VibVoice}, an end-to-end multimodal speech enhancement system for HMWs that uses bone-conducted vibration to guide audio enhancement. VibVoice is designed for mobile deployment, considering on-device sensing, execution latency, and communication overhead.
VibVoice exploits the complementary characteristics of the two modalities: acceleration has a low sampling rate and covers only part of the audible spectrum, but it is largely free of environmental noise. The microphone has a higher sampling rate and covers the full audible spectrum, but it is mixed with ambient sound and competing speech. This combination provides a stable reference for extracting the target speech.
VibVoice uses a DNN to reconstruct clean speech with modality-specific encoders and decoders. The design addresses three technical challenges:

\noindent\textbf{Lack of paired labeled acceleration and audio.}
Collecting a paired multi-modal dataset is labor-intensive. Although there are public datasets of either acceleration or audio, none has the paired data on head-mounted wearables.
Collecting such a paired dataset on a large scale can introduce excessive overheads, such as recruiting diverse volunteers and recording hundreds of hours of audio with annotations.

\noindent\textbf{Fusion of data with different modalities and sample rates.}
Microphone audio has much richer information than acceleration data due to the higher sample rate. As a result, the DNN is prone to overfitting and may ignore the data input of acceleration.
Therefore, model training requires a dedicated strategy to avoid overfitting and to balance the two modalities.

\noindent\textbf{Deployment robustness.}
Deploying deep learning speech enhancement in daily environments is difficult because voices and bone-conduction channels vary across users.
Even though some users may tolerate a rapid data collection phase before use, the amount and quality of the collected data are not guaranteed for training a high-performance model.

To address these challenges, VibVoice is designed based on measurements of bone-conduction effects in real-world data. The main contributions of this chapter are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item VibVoice estimates the bone-conduction function from a limited dataset and uses it to augment paired acceleration and audio based on a large public audio dataset. The augmented paired data supports model training with less device-specific collection.
    \item VibVoice uses a two-branch DNN that recovers speech-related information from low-sampling-rate acceleration, extracts clean speech from contaminated audio with acceleration guidance, and runs on mobile devices with low latency.
    \item VibVoice is evaluated on a two-modal dataset collected with \emph{EarSense}, a custom platform equipped with an accelerometer and microphone for synchronized acceleration and audio recording. The dataset and data-collection design are open-sourced.
\end{itemize}

VibVoice is evaluated on paired acceleration and microphone data from 15 volunteers, using both offline synthetic noise and concurrent noise scenarios.
Among the evaluated methods, the system achieves the strongest enhancement performance, runs with up to 31$\times$ lower mobile latency than two strong baselines, and reduces the amount of required paired data by more than 72$\times$.
A user study with 35 volunteers shows that 87\% prefer VibVoice over the baseline.
