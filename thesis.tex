\begin{abstract}
Human behavior captured by wearables is fundamentally shaped by \textit{user--environment coupling}: user-generated signals and environmental context are physically mixed, structurally coupled, and socially organized. This thesis studies that coupling on commodity earphones and formulates three complementary dimensions for sensing: \textit{differentiation} (isolating user-specific signals from mixtures), \textit{fusion} (extracting informative user--context structure), and \textit{collaboration} (leveraging interactions among multiple users). Across these dimensions, we show that coupling is not merely interference to suppress, but a source of actionable information for robust, privacy-preserving sensing without cameras.

For \textit{differentiation}, we present \textit{VibVoice}, a bone-conduction-guided speech enhancement system. VibVoice fuses microphone audio with IMU-measured skull vibrations in a lightweight two-branch encoder--decoder, using vibration as a user-specific reference to extract target speech. To reduce the need for paired data, we introduce a physics-based cross-modal augmentation method based on measured bone-conduction impulse responses. VibVoice improves PESQ by up to 21\% and SNR by 26\% over strong baselines, uses 72$\times$ less paired data, achieves 4--31$\times$ faster mobile inference, and is preferred by 87\% of 35 participants.

For \textit{fusion}, \textit{EgoLog} provides fine-grained activity logging with commercial earphones. EgoLog separates near-field user sounds from far-field ambient sounds through motion-compensated localization, then brings environmental semantics into the model through contrastive cross-modal learning for context-aware inference. The system supports zero-shot logging across up to 41 categories and improves F1-score by 38\% over ImageBind-LLM.

For \textit{collaboration}, we develop \textit{CoHear}, a conversation-level enhancement system for infrastructure-free multi-earphone networks. A conversation-driven peer-discovery protocol and a bandwidth-efficient extraction model jointly use relay audio and social interaction structure to recover target speech in overlapping scenes. CoHear achieves over 90\% group-formation accuracy, improves speech quality by up to 8.8 dB over state-of-the-art baselines, and runs in real time on mobile devices.

Taken together, these results show that user--environment coupling provides a practical foundation for turning commodity earphones into privacy-preserving sensing platforms.

\end{abstract}

\begin{cabstract}
可穿戴感知中的人類行為理解，本質上受\textit{使用者--環境耦合}支配：使用者訊號與環境資訊在物理上混合、在結構上耦合、在社交中組織。本論文以商用耳機為平臺，將該耦合統一為三類互補機制：\textit{區分}（從混合觀測中分離使用者特異訊號）、\textit{融合}（提取使用者與情境的有效聯合結構）與\textit{協作}（利用多使用者互動提升感知效能）。我們證明，耦合並非僅是需要抑制的干擾，而是實現穩健、隱私友好感知的關鍵資訊來源。

針對\textit{區分}問題，我們提出 \textit{VibVoice} 骨傳導引導語音增強系統。該方法以輕量雙分支編碼器--解碼器融合麥克風與IMU骨振訊號，並將骨振作為使用者專屬參考進行目標語音提取。為緩解配對資料稀缺，我們引入基於實測骨傳導響應的物理約束跨模態增強方法。實驗結果顯示：相較強基線，PESQ最高提升21\%，SNR提升26\%，配對資料需求降低72$\times$，行動端推理加速4--31$\times$；35名受試者中87\%更偏好VibVoice。

針對\textit{融合}問題，我們提出面向細粒度活動記錄的 \textit{EgoLog}。系統通過運動補償聲源定位分離近場使用者聲音與遠場環境聲音，再以對比式跨模態學習將環境語義融合到活動推斷中，實現情境感知的穩健識別。EgoLog在最多41個類別上實現零樣本記錄，F1分數較ImageBind-LLM提升38\%。

針對\textit{協作}問題，我們提出無基礎設施多耳機對話增強系統 \textit{CoHear}。該系統通過對話驅動的鄰居發現協議與帶寬高效的目標會話提取模型，聯合利用中繼音訊與社交互動結構，在多說話人重疊場景下恢復目標語音。真實實驗與模擬表明：群組建構準確率超過90\%，語音品質較最優基線最高提升8.8 dB，並可在行動裝置上即時運行。

綜上，VibVoice、EgoLog與CoHear共同表明：以使用者--環境耦合為統一建模原則，可將商用耳機轉化為實用的隱私友好感知平臺。
\end{cabstract}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis formalizes user--environment coupling as a unified sensing framework on head-mounted wearables, showing that coupling is not merely interference to suppress but a source of structured information. We developed three systems demonstrating this principle across the dimensions of differentiation, fusion, and collaboration. VibVoice uses bone-conducted vibration from the IMU as a user-exclusive reference for speech enhancement. EgoLog combines IMU motion with ambient audio for context-aware activity logging. CoHear enables infrastructure-free collaborative conversation enhancement through multi-earphone coordination.

\section{Future Work}

Certain limitations point to concrete directions for future work. EarSense is an external hardware platform not yet integrated into commercial earphones, and its 1.6~kHz IMU exceeds typical consumer accelerometer rates. All three systems also offload heavy computation to smartphones, introducing latency and privacy trade-offs for continuous deployment. Beyond addressing these gaps, a broader next step is to move beyond task-specific designs toward a unified sensing framework that jointly models differentiation, fusion, and collaboration with richer multimodal inputs and efficient on-device deployment.

The following directions appear especially promising:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Embodied agents on wearables:}
  This thesis focuses on audio and IMU sensing while intentionally avoiding cameras to preserve privacy and generalizability. As privacy-preserving visual sensors (e.g., event cameras, depth sensors on AR glasses) become more common, future work can explore how on-device visual processing complements audio and motion for sound localization, activity recognition, and social interaction---advancing all three coupling dimensions while maintaining the design principles established here. Compared with robotics platforms, head-mounted wearables offer different advantages: they move with the user, fit naturally into daily life, and remain tightly coupled to the user's behavior and context.

  \item \textbf{Proactive user understanding:}
  The systems in this thesis mainly respond to user needs after the sensing task has been defined. With ongoing progress in large language models and on-device intelligence, a next step is to build proactive, personalized assistants on top of user--environment coupling. Systems derived from VibVoice or CoHear could use both explicit instructions and contextual cues to decide when and how to enhance audio---for example, automatically emphasizing the current speaker when a user enters a meeting. This direction can be seen as a tighter integration of the \textit{fusion} and \textit{collaboration} dimensions into the user's daily loop.

  \item \textbf{Deployment and computation:}
  The current systems rely on companion smartphones for the most expensive computation. Communication between wearables and phones adds latency, which is a constraint for applications such as hearing assistance where outputs may need to arrive within 10--20~ms. It also raises privacy concerns because continuous sensing data must leave the wearable. A practical next step is to move more of the pipeline onto earphone-class hardware through compression techniques such as pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, and neural architecture search designed for DSP and MCU platforms. This is essential for scaling the \textit{differentiation} and \textit{fusion} dimensions to always-on scenarios.

  \item \textbf{Unified models and weakly supervised learning:}
  Wearables are well suited to collecting large-scale, in-the-wild multimodal data that reflects real user--environment coupling. Because users generate this data naturally without annotation, much of it will be weakly labeled or unlabeled. An open problem is how to learn robust, transferable representations from such data for wearable sensing tasks. This need is especially clear for sound source localization, where labeled datasets remain limited despite the fact that ear-level devices capture binaural audio and IMU signals continuously in everyday life. Combining self-supervised and weakly supervised learning may lead to shared models and benchmarks that generalize across users, environments, and downstream tasks---providing a foundation for all three coupling dimensions.

  \item \textbf{Long-term impact and human feedback:}
  Long-term use of head-mounted wearables matters beyond data collection. It creates the possibility of personalized sensing systems that adapt to a user's habits, acoustic conditions, and social routines over time. Future work should therefore include long-term, in-the-wild deployments to test how robust these systems remain across different users, devices, environments, and daily routines. Month-scale studies would reveal model drift, changes in user compliance, and practical failure modes that shorter experiments miss. At the same time, long-term deployment raises clear ethical questions around transparency, consent, data governance, and privacy-preserving system design. Addressing these is critical for any practical realization of user--environment coupling across all three dimensions.

\end{itemize}
