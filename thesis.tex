\begin{abstract}
Human behavior captured by wearables is shaped by \textit{user--environment coupling}: user-generated signals and environmental context are physically mixed, structurally related, and socially organized. This thesis studies this coupling on earphone-class head-mounted wearables, including commodity earphones and earphone-form-factor research prototypes. These devices already contain microphones and motion sensors, yet avoid the privacy concerns of always-on cameras. The central argument is that coupling should not be treated only as interference. When modeled explicitly, it becomes structured information for robust, privacy-preserving sensing.

To make this argument concrete, the thesis formulates three complementary dimensions of user--environment coupling: \textit{differentiation}, \textit{fusion}, and \textit{collaboration}. For \textit{differentiation}, we present \textit{VibVoice}, a bone-conduction-guided speech enhancement system. VibVoice fuses microphone audio with IMU-measured skull vibrations in a lightweight two-branch encoder--decoder, using vibration as a user-specific reference to extract target speech. To reduce the need for paired data, we introduce a physics-based cross-modal augmentation method based on measured bone-conduction impulse responses. VibVoice improves PESQ by up to 21\% and SNR by 26\% over strong baselines, uses 72$\times$ less paired data, achieves 4--31$\times$ faster mobile inference, and is preferred by 87\% of 35 participants.

For \textit{fusion}, \textit{EgoLog} provides fine-grained activity logging for audio--motion mobile wearables, with head-mounted devices as the core deployment target for day-long sensing. EgoLog first separates near-field user sounds from far-field ambient sounds through motion-compensated localization, then uses environmental semantics as context through contrastive cross-modal learning. The system supports zero-shot logging across up to 41 categories and improves F1-score by 38\% over ImageBind-LLM.

For \textit{collaboration}, we develop \textit{CoHear}, a conversation-level enhancement system for infrastructure-free multi-earphone networks. A conversation-driven peer-discovery protocol and a bandwidth-efficient extraction model jointly use relay audio and social interaction structure to recover target speech in overlapping scenes. CoHear achieves over 90\% group-formation accuracy, improves speech quality by up to 8.8 dB over representative baselines, and runs in real time on mobile devices.

Together, these systems show how earphone-class head-mounted wearables can become privacy-preserving sensing platforms by modeling, rather than ignoring, the physical, contextual, and social coupling around the user.

\end{abstract}

\begin{cabstract}
可穿戴裝置所捕獲的人類行為受到\textit{使用者--環境耦合}的影響：使用者產生的訊號與環境脈絡在物理上混合，在結構上關聯，並在社會互動中組織。本論文以商用耳機為研究平台。耳機已經廣泛部署，並配備麥克風與運動感測器，同時避免了連續攝影所帶來的隱私問題。本文的核心觀點是，使用者--環境耦合不應只被視為需要抑制的干擾；當它被顯式建模時，也可以成為穩健、隱私友好感知的結構化資訊來源。

圍繞這一觀點，本文提出使用者--環境耦合的三個互補維度：\textit{區分}、\textit{融合}與\textit{協作}。在\textit{區分}維度上，本文提出\textit{VibVoice}，一個由骨傳導訊號引導的語音增強系統。VibVoice 將麥克風音訊與 IMU 測得的顱骨振動輸入輕量級雙分支編碼器--解碼器網路，利用振動訊號作為使用者專屬參考來提取目標語音。為降低對配對訓練資料的需求，本文基於實測骨傳導脈衝響應提出物理啟發的跨模態資料增強方法。實驗結果表明，VibVoice 相比強基線最高提升 21\% 的 PESQ 和 26\% 的 SNR，僅需 1/72 的配對資料，在行動端實現 4--31$\times$ 更快的推理速度，並獲得 35 名參與者中 87\% 的偏好。

在\textit{融合}維度上，本文提出\textit{EgoLog}，一個基於商用耳機的細粒度活動日誌系統。EgoLog 首先透過運動補償聲源定位區分近場使用者聲音與遠場環境聲音，再透過對比式跨模態學習將環境語義作為脈絡引入推理過程。該系統支援最多 41 個類別的零樣本日誌生成，並相比 ImageBind-LLM 提升 38\% 的 F1 分數。

在\textit{協作}維度上，本文提出\textit{CoHear}，一個面向無基礎設施多耳機網路的會話級語音增強系統。CoHear 透過會話驅動的鄰居發現協議和頻寬高效的語音提取模型，聯合利用中繼音訊與社會互動結構，在重疊說話場景中恢復目標語音。CoHear 實現超過 90\% 的群組形成準確率，相比現有方法最高提升 8.8 dB 的語音品質，並可在行動裝置上即時運行。

綜上，VibVoice、EgoLog 與 CoHear 共同表明：透過建模而非忽略使用者周圍的物理、脈絡與社會耦合，日常商用耳機可以成為實用的隱私友好感知平台。

\end{cabstract}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis studies how head-mounted wearables can sense human behavior when the user and the surrounding environment are tightly coupled. Rather than treating this coupling only as interference, the thesis models it as a source of structure. The resulting framework organizes wearable sensing into three dimensions: \textit{differentiation}, which separates user-specific signals from mixtures; \textit{fusion}, which uses environmental context to interpret user behavior; and \textit{collaboration}, which uses interactions among multiple users and devices.

The three systems developed in this thesis instantiate these dimensions on earphone-class head-mounted wearables. VibVoice shows that bone-conducted vibration measured by an earphone IMU can serve as a user-specific reference for speech enhancement. EgoLog shows that ambient sound and motion can be jointly modeled to produce fine-grained, context-aware activity logs on mobile and head-mounted devices. CoHear shows that conversation structure and peer-device observations can be used to enhance speech in overlapping multi-user scenes without fixed infrastructure. Together, these systems support the central claim of the thesis: head-mounted wearables become more capable when they model the physical, contextual, and social structure around the user, rather than assuming that the user can be sensed independently from the environment.

\section{Future Work}

The systems in this thesis also reveal several limits of current head-mounted sensing. VibVoice relies on EarSense, an external hardware platform whose 1.6~kHz IMU sampling rate is higher than that of many commercial earphones. EgoLog and CoHear depend on model components that still require careful adaptation across users, devices, and environments. All three systems offload substantial computation to companion smartphones, which introduces latency and exposes continuous sensing data outside the wearable. These limits point to the next stage of the research: moving from task-specific systems toward unified, efficient, and long-term deployable models of user--environment coupling.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Embodied sensing on head-mounted wearables:}
  This thesis focuses on audio and IMU sensing and intentionally avoids cameras for privacy and deployability. Future head-mounted platforms may include more privacy-preserving visual sensors, such as event cameras or low-resolution depth sensors on AR glasses. A natural next step is to study how these sensors can complement audio and motion without changing the privacy assumptions that motivate this thesis. Unlike robots or room-scale sensing systems, head-mounted devices move with the user and observe the world from the user's own position. This makes them a useful platform for embodied assistants that understand speech, activity, attention, and social context from a first-person perspective.

  \item \textbf{Proactive user understanding:}
  The systems in this thesis respond to sensing tasks that are defined in advance: enhance this speech signal, recognize this activity, or recover this conversation. Future systems could use the same coupling framework to infer when assistance is needed. For example, an earphone could detect that the wearer has entered a noisy meeting, identify the active conversation group, and adjust speech enhancement without an explicit command. This direction requires models that combine explicit user intent with contextual cues, and it will likely connect the \textit{fusion} and \textit{collaboration} dimensions more tightly.

  \item \textbf{Deployment and computation:}
  The current systems rely on companion smartphones for the most expensive computation. This is practical for prototypes, but it adds communication latency and weakens privacy because raw or intermediate sensing data must leave the earphone. Applications such as hearing assistance are especially sensitive to delay. Future work should move more inference onto earphone-class hardware through model compression, quantization, knowledge distillation, and neural architecture search for DSP and MCU platforms. Efficient on-device models are essential if differentiation, fusion, and collaboration are to run continuously in daily life.

  \item \textbf{Unified models and weakly supervised learning:}
  The three systems in this thesis are designed separately, but their inputs and failure modes overlap. A broader research direction is to learn shared representations from in-the-wild audio, motion, and interaction data. Most of this data will be weakly labeled or unlabeled, because users will generate it naturally during daily life. Self-supervised and weakly supervised learning may therefore be necessary for building models that transfer across users, devices, and environments. This is particularly important for tasks such as sound localization and conversation understanding, where labeled ear-level datasets remain limited.

  \item \textbf{Long-term impact and human feedback:}
  Long-term deployment is necessary for understanding whether user--environment coupling remains stable outside short experiments. Month-scale studies could reveal model drift, changes in user behavior, device wear, battery constraints, and failure cases that laboratory evaluations miss. They would also make it possible to study personalization: a system could adapt to a user's voice, routines, acoustic environments, and social patterns over time. Such deployment must be paired with clear mechanisms for consent, transparency, data governance, and user correction. Without these mechanisms, technically capable sensing systems will be difficult to justify in everyday life.

\end{itemize}
